## Game plan for Billy

### 1. Lead with the wedge and the outcome

Open with something close to:

> “We are not building another destination chatbot. Alfred helps families move from uncertain inspiration to a trustworthy, bookable plan—fast. The product is built around the hard part AI demos skip: understanding a family’s constraints, making tradeoffs explicit, grounding recommendations, and getting the family confidently to action.”

Then show **one family journey** end to end:

1. messy family inputs and constraints;
2. personalized shortlist with reasons and tradeoffs;
3. itinerary that is explainable and editable;
4. booking/action handoff;
5. feedback or post-trip signal that improves the next recommendation.

Use the words **trust, authorization, accountability, and action** only when tied to a concrete product behavior or metric.

### 2. Position Alfred against generic AI travel-planner noise

| Generic planner claim | Alfred’s sharper answer |
|---|---|
| “We generate itineraries.” | “We reduce family decision friction and move the user to a confident, bookable decision.” |
| “Personalized by AI.” | “Personalization is constrained by family composition, risk tolerance, pace, budget, accessibility, and explicit tradeoffs—not just a prompt.” |
| “Agentic booking.” | “We earn authorization progressively; recommendations are grounded, reviewable, and the user remains in control.” |
| “More content.” | “More confidence: fewer bad-fit options, clear reasons, and a path to action.” |
| “Model advantage.” | “Workflow, trust, family preference data, outcome/booking feedback, and distribution—not a claim that our base model is unique.” |

Do not attack competitors by name. Say the category is noisy and explain the durable layer Alfred is building.

### 3. Questions to ask Gilad

Prioritize questions that invite his thesis rather than perform research back at him:

1. “You have seen trip planning evolve from Utrip through today’s agentic wave. What separates the travel AI products that become durable businesses from the ones that remain demos?”
2. “Where do you think the trusted position sits: consumer interface, agent identity/credentialing, supply-side workflow, or the transaction layer?”
3. “For a family-travel product, what evidence would convince you that trust is a moat rather than just good UX?”
4. “What operator or supply integrations create real defensibility, and which are distractions at our stage?”
5. “How would you measure whether an AI travel product is creating value beyond itinerary generation—conversion, repeat planning, margin, attach rate, or something else?”
6. “What is your current view on consumer-facing trip planners versus behind-the-scenes/operator applications?”
7. “What would make Alfred look too narrow, and what would make it too broad?”
8. “If you were pressure-testing Alfred over the next 90 days, which two proofs would you demand?”
9. “Assuming the product and early evidence are compelling, where do you believe you can be most useful—capital, product strategy, supply/industry access, or board-level pattern recognition?”

The critical discovery question is: **“What would you need to see to believe this is a category-defining trust-and-action platform rather than an AI wrapper?”**

## Likely objections and disciplined responses

### “Foundation models will make this feature free.”
**Response:** “We agree that generation is commoditizing. Our moat is the trusted decision workflow: family-specific context, grounded recommendations, explicit tradeoffs, authorization, booking/action conversion, and feedback loops. We measure the outcome, not the novelty of the generated prose.” Then give one metric and one product example.

### “Why won’t Google/Booking/Expedia or an OTA copy it?”
**Response:** “They have distribution and supply; we do not pretend otherwise. Our wedge is the high-friction family decision and the trust context that makes a recommendation useful before a user is ready to transact. We need to prove repeat use, conversion, and a data/feedback loop that improves faster in this segment.” Acknowledge the risk; do not claim incumbents cannot copy features.

### “Consumer travel acquisition is expensive.”
**Response:** Explain the initial audience, channel, activation event, referral/share loop, and the point at which Alfred earns or captures booking value. If any of those are still hypotheses, label them as such and state the experiment underway.

### “Travel has bad data, fragmented supply, and liability.”
**Response:** Describe grounding, freshness, source visibility, uncertainty handling, human/user confirmation, and what Alfred will not autonomously do. Be precise about where the user authorizes action and how errors are surfaced.

### “Family travel is a niche.”
**Response:** Frame family as a severe, repeated decision problem and a focused wedge—not the ceiling. The expansion logic should be adjacent cohorts/complex trips and supply relationships, but do not present a giant TAM slide as proof of product-market fit.

### “The itinerary is not the business.”
**Response:** Agree. “The itinerary is the visible artifact. The business is reducing uncertainty and converting intent into confident, monetizable action.” Show the handoff and economics.

### “What exactly is proprietary?”
**Response:** Answer in layers: (1) family preference/constraint representation, (2) feedback from accepted/rejected recommendations and real outcomes, (3) workflow/integration know-how, and (4) trusted brand/distribution. Do not call generic prompts, model access, or scraped content proprietary.

## What not to say

- Do not say “we are the first,” “no one else can do this,” or “AI agents will book everything” without evidence.
- Do not lead with model names, prompt quality, or a generic “agentic” demo.
- Do not imply that Gilad’s board roles or Utrip history guarantee a strategic fit or investment.
- Do not claim a check size, timing, mandate, or active interest that is not public.
- Do not hide the OTA/incumbent risk, CAC, supply/data limitations, or booking economics.
- Do not overuse references to his public content (“I watched your talk…”). One natural reference is enough; let him articulate his own view.
- Do not present “trust” as a slogan. Tie it to authorization, provenance, transparent tradeoffs, error recovery, and measurable conversion/retention.
- Do not promise fully autonomous booking if Alfred currently requires confirmation or cannot guarantee inventory/price accuracy.
