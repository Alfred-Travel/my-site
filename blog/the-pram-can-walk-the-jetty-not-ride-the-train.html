The pram can walk the jetty, not ride the train

For a family with a toddler, the Margaret River region is a run of yes-and-no rules about the pram. The jetty says yes on foot and no on the train. The caves say no to prams and to backpack carriers. Plan the carrier, not just the ticket.

Busselton Jetty says prams, walkers, wheelchairs and mobility scooters are welcome on the jetty. It also says prams and strollers, including compact folding ones, can’t go on the jetty train or into the Underwater Observatory, and it recommends a baby carrier.

That isn’t a contradiction. They are two different tickets on the same structure, and the region’s caves and lighthouses add rules of their own. A toddler day here is planned around which adult carries what.

The signal: the rules are per ticket, not per place

We read Busselton Jetty’s admission, jetty train and Underwater Observatory pages, Capes Foundation’s pages for Mammoth, Lake, Jewel and Ngilgi caves and the Cape Leeuwin and Cape Naturaliste lighthouses, and Parks and Wildlife Service pages for Leeuwin-Naturaliste National Park, on 6 November 2026.

At the jetty, the train and the observatory say no prams, and so does the Marine Discovery Centre. The walk says yes. For a A$10 deposit, Busselton Jetty says you can lock your pram to the jetty while you ride. Children under 3 need an infant ticket and sit on an adult’s lap on the train.

What breaks when the pram is the plan

The train: a pram you meant to push onto the train has to go back to the car or be locked up. Busselton Jetty suggests arriving 15 minutes before departure to collect tickets. That is also the time to sort out the pram.

The cave entrance: Capes Foundation lists no prams at Mammoth, Lake and Jewel caves. At all four of its caves, it says front-worn baby carriers are fine but backpack baby carriers are not permitted, and asks you to leave backpacks in the car. A family with only a hiking carrier is stuck at the door.

The stairs: the carrier goes up and down with an adult. Capes Foundation lists 370 steps at Mammoth Cave, 350 at Ngilgi, 325 stairs at Lake Cave and 250 at Jewel Cave. Busselton Jetty lists six flights of ten stairs in the observatory, with a lift.

The tower: at Cape Leeuwin, children must be at least 4 to climb the lighthouse, and Capes Foundation lists pram access for the grounds and museum only. One adult climbs with the older child while the other stays below with the toddler. Under 4s visit the grounds and museum free.

Where the pram does go

Busselton Jetty says prams are welcome on the 1.841 km jetty walk, about 25 minutes each way. Parks and Wildlife says the 3.4 km sealed path from Sugarloaf Rock to Cape Naturaliste is assisted wheelchair and pram accessible, with boardwalks, lookouts and benches. Those are the two pram walks on our route.

Four checks for a toddler day

Carrier, pram, tickets, split

Carrier: pack a front-worn carrier. A backpack carrier won’t get you into the caves.

Pram: before you leave the car, decide whether the pram stays in the boot, gets locked at the jetty, or comes on a walk.

Tickets: book an infant ticket for an under-3 on the jetty train, and check at checkout whether a cave wants a free ticket for a child under 4.

Split: where a tower or cave has an age or stair limit, decide which adult goes and which stays with the toddler.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list Busselton Jetty or Capes Foundation publishes.

Practical next step

Before you drive south, list each ticketed stop and write one word next to it: pram, carrier or split. If a stop says carrier, check it is the front-worn kind. Then pack the car in that order. For the full plan, see our 5-day Margaret River family itinerary from Perth.
