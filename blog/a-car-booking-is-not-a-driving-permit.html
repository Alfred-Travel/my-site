A car booking is not a driving permit

A hire-car confirmation says a car will be waiting. In Japan, the counter still won’t hand over the keys until every driver shows the right paper permit, and the child seats are in the booking.

A day card that says “collect hire car, drive to Churaumi” assumes the keys come with the booking. In Japan they come with documents. Each driver needs a 1949-format International Driving Permit, their physical Australian licence and, at some companies, a passport. A child under six needs a child seat. Write the documents on the card, not just the car.

The signal: one permit format, on paper, before you leave

On 15 October 2026 we checked the official pages. Smartraveller says that to drive in Japan you need a Japanese licence, or a valid International Driving Permit (IDP) together with a current Australian driver’s licence. It also says to get the IDP before you leave Australia. Tokyo’s Metropolitan Police say Japan only recognises permits issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention. Permits under other conventions, including the 1969 Vienna Convention, are not valid there.

The Australian Automobile Association (AAA) says Australian IDPs follow that 1949 format and come as a physical book. There is no digital version. The permit is valid for 12 months from issue, or until your licence expires if that comes first. It only counts when you carry your physical photocard licence with it, because digital driver’s licences aren’t valid overseas. The AAA and its state and territory motoring clubs are the only authorised issuers in Australia.

The hire desk adds its own layer. OTS Rent-a-car, which hires out cars in Okinawa, asks for the IDP, the original licence and a passport. It won’t accept photocopies, digital versions or expired documents, and your rental date has to fall inside the permit’s validity.

What breaks when the permit is treated as a booking detail

The licence on the phone: a digital licence can be used to apply for an IDP in Australia, but the AAA says it may not be recognised overseas. Bring the photocard.

The licence bought online: the AAA warns that “International Driver’s Licence” products, often sold as a digital permit valid for three years, are fraudulent. A real Australian IDP carries the AAA logo and official stamp, and lasts no more than 12 months.

The late order: the AAA says online orders are delivered in 3–5 business days. If you fly within five business days, it suggests applying in person at a motoring club branch, and some branches need an appointment.

The second driver: if two adults plan to share a two-hour drive, both need the documents. Luana Rent a Car in Naha says it checks the licences of everyone who will drive.

The missing seat: Japan’s National Police Agency says drivers must not carry a child under six without a child seat. OTS and Nippon Rent-A-Car say they may refuse the rental if you haven’t booked one or brought your own, even when the car is reserved.

Four checks before a driving day goes on the plan

Treat the car as a document check

Which permit the country accepts: read the rule on the destination’s government or police page, not a rental listing. For Japan it is a 1949 Geneva Convention IDP plus your home licence. Rules differ by country. The AAA notes that Vietnam doesn’t recognise Australian IDPs at all.

Which documents each driver carries: the IDP book, the physical photocard licence and a passport. Check that the permit is still valid on the day you collect the car.

How long you have to get it: count business days from today to your flight. If postage won’t make it, plan an in-person visit to a motoring club branch.

Who rides, and what happens without the car: book a child seat for every child under six with the car itself. Then write down the no-car version of the day. In Okinawa, Churaumi Aquarium says the express bus from Naha takes about three hours, against about two hours by car.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a checklist any government or hire company publishes. Rules and company terms change, so reopen the official pages before you book the car.

Practical next step

For every day on your plan that needs a car, write three lines: who drives, which documents each driver carries, and what the day looks like without the car. If any line is blank, the driving day is still a hope. Fill it in before you pay for the car.
