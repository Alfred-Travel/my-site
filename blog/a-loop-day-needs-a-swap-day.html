A loop day needs a swap day

A circular day out by train, cable car, ropeway, boat and bus works only if every link runs. When one link can stop on the day, give the loop a neighbour it can trade places with.

Plan a trip Browse the blog

2026-10-21 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

Hakone’s classic day from Tokyo is a loop: mountain railway to Gora, cable car to Sōunzan, ropeway over Owakudani to Lake Ashi, pirate ship to Moto-Hakone, bus back. On a family plan it looks like one item. It is five links in a row, and the ropeway in the middle can stop for wind, thunder or volcanic gas on the morning you planned it.

The fix is not a better forecast. It is a second day next to the loop, with nothing fixed in it, so the two can trade places the night before.

The signal: a quiet volcano, a ropeway that can still stop

On 21 October 2026 we opened the Hakone Ropeway’s pages on Hakone Navi, the Odakyu Hakone Group’s official site. The ropeway says it stops for wind of 30 metres a second or more, extreme weather, thunder, approaching typhoons and mechanical trouble, and for an inspection every year. It runs from 9 am to 5 pm between February and November, so a stopped morning has little afternoon left.

It also posts a live volcanic-gas reading. Its Japanese page says a gas suspension is decided from several measurements, and that gas levels have no direct link to the volcanic alert level. The Japan Meteorological Agency has kept Hakone at Level 1, the lowest, since 7 October 2019.

The same day, the Hakone Tourist Association’s booking page for the guided walk into Owakudani’s steaming valley showed all four of that day’s sessions cancelled for safety reasons. The alert level had not moved.

Some travellers should not ride at all. The ropeway’s notice says people with asthma, bronchial or lung conditions, heart conditions or a pacemaker cannot ride to Owakudani Station. It asks pregnant travellers, families with babies and small children, and older travellers to think it through first.

Planned stops are published months ahead. Owakudani to Sōunzan closes from 7 to 24 December 2026, and Togendai to Owakudani from 12 January to 26 February 2027. Replacement buses run about every 15 minutes, and the operator expects them to be very crowded.

Cairns has the same shape. The Kuranda day goes up on the Kuranda Scenic Railway and down on the Skyrail Rainforest Cableway, or the other way round. Skyrail’s closure page lists a Monday and Tuesday shutdown every week of October 2026 for an upgrade, and says it runs only a Kuranda to Barron Falls loop until 1 November. The railway has its own maintenance dates, including 2 to 6 November 2026.

What breaks when the loop is planned as one item

No later slot on the same day: if the ropeway is stopped at 9 am and opens again at noon, the boat, the shrine and the bus home still have to fit before evening.

A fixed booking the day after: a timed ticket, a dinner or a hotel move on the next day means the loop has nowhere to go.

The wrong signal: a Level 1 volcano tells you nothing about today’s gas reading or wind. The ropeway’s own status does.

A way home that needs the stopped link: on the far side of the lake, Hakone Navi lists a Hakone Tozan bus from Togendai to Hakone-Yumoto through Sengoku and Miyanoshita, and a highway bus across the lake with few departures. Know which one you would take before you need it.

The traveller who can’t go: for a child with asthma, the gas notice rules out the ropeway to Owakudani whatever the weather.

Four checks before a loop day goes on the plan

Pair the loop with a swap day

Name the fragile link: in Hakone it is the ropeway; at Kuranda it is Skyrail. It is the one link the rest of the day is built around.

Know what stops it, and where that is posted: wind, thunder, gas or planned works. Hakone Navi shows live status for the Odakyu-affiliated lines only; Skyrail lists closures on its own notices page.

Put a swap day next to it: a day with no timed bookings that uses different links, such as Lake Ashi by bus and boat.

Decide the night before, and write the way home: check the status at dinner, swap if needed, and write the route home that doesn’t use the fragile link.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a checklist the ropeway, the Hakone Tourist Association, the Japan Meteorological Agency or Skyrail publish. Status, gas readings and closure dates change, so reopen the official pages before you go.

Where Alfred fits

Alfred plans from a travelling profile, so the ages it plans for come from the travellers on that profile. On web Lets Go, Holiday and Multi-city both take a day pace: Slow fills only an afternoon slot, Moderate a morning and an afternoon, and Active adds an optional evening. Alfred doesn’t check ropeway status, volcanic gas or maintenance notices while it writes the day.

When the itinerary is ACTIVE, you can edit an item’s title, times and notes, so “Ropeway loop: check Hakone Navi at 7.30; if stopped, swap with Thursday” can sit on the card. In Hey Alfred, you can ask to swap two full days, such as “Swap Wednesday and Thursday”, and you confirm before it is applied.

A day with a flight can’t be swapped that way. Our Hakone from Tokyo itinerary pairs Day 3 and Day 4 like this. Book opens a partner checkout. It doesn’t know whether the ropeway is running.

Practical next step

Find the loop day on your plan. Next to it, write the fragile link, where its status is posted, and the day it can swap with. If no day can swap, move the fixed booking, not the loop.
