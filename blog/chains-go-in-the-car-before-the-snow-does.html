Chains go in the car before the snow does

In Yosemite, chain controls are common from November to March, and whenever one is posted a hire car must carry tyre chains, whatever the forecast said. Buy them before you leave the city, learn the sign, and keep a day that doesn’t need the high road.

A hire car feels like the flexible part of a mountain trip. In Yosemite, from November to March, it comes with a rule most families don’t plan for: when a chain control is posted, every vehicle must carry tyre chains, including four-wheel drives and rental cars. The park says to expect those controls even if you only visit Yosemite Valley and ride the free shuttle.

The fix isn’t to avoid winter. It is to put chains in the car at the start, read the sign that tells you whether to fit them, and keep a version of the day that doesn’t need the mountain road.

The signal: the rule rides with the car

We read the National Park Service’s Yosemite pages on tyre chains, driving, current conditions and public transport, and the YARTS Highway 140 timetable, on 26 October 2026. The chain page says controls are often in force inside and outside the park from November through March, less often in October and April, and rarely in September or May.

The requirement is about possession first. Inside a designated chain control area, all vehicles must have chains or cables on board, even when the sign exempts them from fitting. The park says conditions dictate the controls, so they can’t be predicted ahead of time, and that the rule is strictly enforced, with citations of up to US$5,000.

The car itself doesn’t solve it. The park says rental companies generally don’t provide chains, that rental cars aren’t exempt even with all-season tyres, and that chains are hard to rent and usually bought. A limited selection is sold at the Village Garage in Yosemite Valley and the Wawona fuel station, and they must match your tyre size.

Not every road is the same. Because higher roads get more snow, the park says chains are needed more often on the Wawona Road (Highway 41), the Big Oak Flat Road (Highway 120 from the west) and Badger Pass Road than on the El Portal Road (Highway 140), the Valley roads and the Hetch Hetchy Road.

What breaks when the plan treats chains as a winter-only problem

The hire car is assumed to be ready: four-wheel drive, all-season tyres and a rental agreement don’t remove the need to carry chains.

The sign is read as yes or no: the park describes three levels. R-1 lets cars, pickups and SUVs under 6,000 pounds with snow tyres on two drive wheels skip fitting. R-2 needs four-wheel drive and snow tyres on all four wheels, under 6,500 pounds. R-3 has no exceptions.

The heavy car surprises you: the park notes that many battery-electric trucks and some large electric SUVs weigh more than those limits and must fit chains even with snow tyres.

The wrong thing is bought: plastic straps and other emergency “chains” don’t meet the requirement. Cables and tyre socks do.

The day has no other version: a grove or viewpoint day on a higher road has nowhere to go when that road is under chain control.

Four checks before the car leaves the city

Chains, sign, road, plan B

Buy chains that fit: check the tyre size on the hire car, then buy chains or cables for it before you reach the mountains, and practise fitting them once.

Know your R-level: note your car’s drive wheels, tyres and weight, so you can read an R-1, R-2 or R-3 sign without guessing.

Call the road line: the park’s line, 209/372-0200 (then 1, 1), covers roads inside Yosemite; Caltrans, 800/427-7623, covers California roads outside.

Keep a lower-road day: give the day on a higher road a Valley version, or a bus version that doesn’t need chains at all.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the park’s pages above. They aren’t a list the park publishes. Chain controls, road status and timetables change, so check the park’s pages and the road line on the morning you drive.

There is a no-chains option. The park suggests parking at a YARTS stop on Highway 140 outside the park, such as Mariposa or El Portal, before any chain control, and riding YARTS in. Its Highway 140 winter timetable runs from 1 October 2026 to 28 May 2027, with no service on Thanksgiving, Christmas Day, New Year’s Day or Easter Sunday.

Practical next step

Find the driving days on your plan. On each card, write the road you will use, whether the park says it needs chains more or less often, your car’s R-level, the road line number, and the day you would do instead. Then put the chains in the car.
