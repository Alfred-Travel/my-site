A dingo fence still has a gate

On K’gari, the dingo plan is made before anyone leaves the room: who closes the gate, which adult is within arm’s reach of which child, where the food stays, and what everyone does if a dingo comes close.

2026-10-30 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

Four checks for a family on K’gari: shut every gate, keep each child within an adult’s reach outside the fence, keep food off the lakeshore and the track, and practise the response if a dingo comes close. These are Alfred’s recommendations, not a Queensland Parks list.

A fenced resort on K’gari feels like a solved problem. Kingfisher Bay Resort says it sits within a dingo fence. On 27 October 2026, Queensland Parks posted an alert (alert 27166) that a dingo was likely to be inside the Kingfisher Bay Resort and Village fenced area, seen loitering in places including the resort area, and asked visitors to close all gates securely. The alert applies from 27 October to 2 November 2026, so it may have lapsed by the time you read this; check the Queensland Parks alerts page.

The fix isn’t to stay indoors. It is to treat the fence as one layer, keep the children within arm’s reach outside it, and agree what everyone does before a dingo comes close.

The signal: a fence works only when the gate is shut

We read the Queensland Parks pages on dingo safety, dingo interactions and dingo management on K’gari, six current park alerts, and Kingfisher Bay Resort’s FAQ and self-guided walks pages on 30 October 2026. The Parks pages blocked our usual fetch, so we read them in a full browser.

The Parks dingo management page quotes a 2012 review: fencing has been effective at eliminating serious incidents inside fenced areas, but the potential remains as long as gates are still left open. Pedestrian gates near the dingo grids have spring-loaded closers so they shut quickly.

The season matters too. A Parks alert lists 1 September to 30 November 2026 as wongari (dingo) rearing and emergence season, when pups leave their dens and adults teach them to hunt. It asks visitors to give dingoes space and to slow down when driving.

What breaks when the fence is the whole plan

The gate is propped: a gate left open lets a dingo in. The 27 October alert asks visitors to close all gates securely and report any fence or gate damage to dingo.ranger@detsi.qld.gov.au. It says QPWS is inspecting potential entry points.

The child is a few metres ahead: Parks says to always stay within arm’s reach of children, even small teenagers, and never to leave them unattended at beaches, lakes or on walking tracks, not even by a few metres or for a few minutes. It says children’s size doesn’t intimidate a dingo as much as an adult’s.

Someone runs: Parks says running or jogging can trigger a negative dingo interaction. It also says not to expect children to remember what to do if a dingo comes close.

Lunch goes to the lake: at Lake McKenzie, Parks says food and drinks other than water are not allowed at the lakeshore, because popular sites attract dingoes. Feeding or disturbing a dingo on K’gari carries a maximum fine of A$28,495.

Four checks before anyone walks out the gate

Gate, reach, food, response

Shut the gate: every gate, every time, and tell a ranger or Reception about any damage. Inside the fence, keep children within sight; a Parks alert on 27 October 2026 said a dingo was likely inside.

Assign the reach: one adult per young child, within arm’s reach, everywhere outside a fence. Kingfisher Bay Resort asks walkers to tell rangers or Reception their route and walk in groups of at least three.

Keep food in the room: no snacks at the lakeshore or on the track. Only water goes to Lake McKenzie.

Practise the response: Parks says if you feel threatened, face the dingo, stand back to back if in pairs, yell for help, calmly back away to a fenced area or vehicle, and never run. Then report it on (07) 4127 9150.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list Queensland Parks or the resort publishes. Parks suggests practising arm’s-reach walking with children at home before you reach the island.

It doesn’t know where the dingoes are.

Practical next step

Before you fly, read the current Queensland Parks alerts for K’gari. On each day card, write which adult holds which child’s hand outside the fence, where the food stays, and who closes the gate. Then practise the back-away with the children at home.
