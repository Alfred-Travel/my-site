AI travel planning needs a decision screen, not another chat thread

AI can understand a travel request quickly. Travellers still need a visible, editable route they can challenge before they book.

Home / Blog / Travel notes

Recent coverage from Skift and PhocusWire points to a useful distinction: AI can make the first answer faster without making the final decision easier.

The chat ends when the decisions begin

Picture a family arriving in London at 7:40 in the morning. Their AI assistant has produced a beautiful list: the Tower, a West End show, a market, two restaurants, and a day trip to Bath.

It has not answered the questions the family actually has. Which hotel base keeps the first day short? What happens if the flight is late? Is the show worth crossing the city for after a long transfer? Which day can disappear without breaking the trip?

The answer is fluent. The trip is still undecided.

A travel plan needs somewhere to land

Chat is a useful place to explore. It is a poor place to keep every decision. Important details disappear up the thread, alternatives get buried under new suggestions, and a small change to the hotel can quietly damage three later days.

A decision screen gives the plan a surface. It shows the route, the bases, the demanding days, the flexible days, and the assumptions that still need checking. The traveller can disagree with it without having to start the conversation again.

Make the trade-off visible

One more attraction can cost a whole evening

A route should show the price of an extra stop in time and energy, not just place another pin on the map. Families often choose a shorter day when the cost is visible.

The hotel is part of the route

A room near the wrong station can turn every morning into a transfer. The useful question is not only whether a hotel looks good. It is what the base does to the days around it.

A flexible day is not an empty day

Weather, fatigue, delays, and changed priorities are normal parts of travel. A protected day gives the family somewhere to put those changes without rebuilding the whole itinerary.

Confirmation belongs beside the suggestion

Opening hours, rail times, entry rules, prices, cancellation terms, and availability should be marked as things to confirm. They should not be made to look settled because the sentence sounds confident.

The best planning tool lets you argue with it

Move the hotel. Remove the museum. Make the transfer day gentler. Keep the beach and lose the second neighbourhood. A good planning system should make those changes cheap.

That is the difference between an AI answer and an editable trip. The answer tries to finish the conversation. The trip needs a place where people can keep making decisions together.
