Down is only half the walk

A walk below a rim or down a stairway comes back up the same way, or by a ride that stops running. Plan the return before the first step down.

Plan a trip Browse the blog

2026-10-20 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

A family day card that says “South Kaibab Trail to Ooh Aah Point, 1.8 miles, 1–2 hours” looks finished. It hides the shape of the walk. The first half is downhill and feels easy. The second half is the climb back to a rim at about 2,200 metres, with children who spent their energy on the way down.

Before a below-the-rim walk or a long stairway goes on the plan, write down three things: how long the way back takes, who sets its pace, and what carries you up if you don’t walk.

The signal: the official pages all warn about the second half

On 20 October 2026 we opened the National Park Service’s pages for the South Kaibab Trail at Grand Canyon. Its trail sheet says the steepness of the trail is “very misleading on the way down” and tells hikers to “plan on taking twice as long to hike up as it took to hike down”. It also asks groups to let the weakest hiker set the pace.

The park’s summer Hike Smart page describes hikers “lured by initially easy downhill hiking”, and its day-hiking page is blunt: there are no easy trails into or out of the Grand Canyon.

Even the numbers move. One park page puts Ooh Aah Point 600 feet below the trailhead; another gives the elevation change as 790 feet. At Walnut Canyon National Monument near Flagstaff, the Island Trail “descends 185 vertical feet into the canyon, and returns the same way”, and three park pages give its stairs as 240, 273 and 736. The vertical drop and the one-hour round trip are the steady figures. The stair count is not something to plan on.

Australia has the same shape of walk. NSW National Parks says the Giant Stairway at Katoomba descends 998 stairs from Echo Point. The longer walk from Echo Point to Scenic World via the stairway is 4.7 km one way, 2 to 3 hours and Grade 4, and many walkers ride the Scenic Railway back up.

NSW National Parks says the last train up is at 3.50 pm; a NSW Government page says 4.50 pm. Scenic World’s own page explains both: it closes at 4 pm on weekdays and 5 pm on weekends, NSW school holidays and public holidays, and last rides leave the valley 10 minutes before closing. The way up is a timetable that changes with the day of the week.

On Tuesday 20 October 2026, NSW National Parks also listed the Furber Steps, Federal Pass and Giant Stairs as closed for a helicopter operation.

What breaks when only the way down is planned

The round trip read as one easy number: “1–2 hours” for Ooh Aah Point covers the whole walk. If the way down takes 30 minutes, the park’s rule of thumb means about an hour for the way up.

The youngest walker on the climb: the pace that matters is the slowest person going uphill at altitude, not the average adult going down.

The ride that stopped: Scenic World’s last rides leave the valley 10 minutes before closing. At Grand Canyon there is no private parking at the South Kaibab Trailhead, so the return depends on the Kaibab Rim shuttle, which in autumn 2026 ran until an hour after sunset.

The water that wasn’t there: the park says there is no water on the South Kaibab Trail, and after the 29 August 2026 flood it turned off all water inside the canyon.

Four checks before a down-first walk goes on the plan

Write the way back before you go down

Which half is uphill: mark whether the walk goes down first. Most rim and stairway walks do, so the hard half comes last.

How long the climb takes: use the operator’s own rule where one exists. For the South Kaibab Trail, the park says twice the descent time. Write a turnaround time on the card, not just a start time.

Who sets the pace: plan the climb at the speed of the slowest walker, as the park advises.

What carries you up, and until when: a train, a shuttle or your own legs. Write the last departure for that day of the week, and check that day’s track closures.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a checklist the National Park Service, NSW National Parks or Scenic World publish. Trails, timetables and closures change, so reopen the official pages before you go.

Where Alfred fits

Practical next step

For every walk on your plan that goes down before it comes back, add one line: the turnaround time, the slowest walker, and the last ride up on that day. If you can’t fill the line, move the walk to a morning with nothing booked after it.
