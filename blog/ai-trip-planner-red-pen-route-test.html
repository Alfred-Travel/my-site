The traveller problem

A polished route can still fail the red-pen test

AI makes it easy to assemble a convincing family holiday in seconds. The hard part is spotting friction that a tidy list hides: a cross-city transfer after a long flight, a hotel far from the first morning, or three “must-do” activities competing for the same afternoon.

That is the useful tension behind the PhocusWire signal that travellers are ready for AI agents while travel companies are still working out how to serve them responsibly. For a traveller, readiness should mean a faster first draft that is easier to inspect.

Use three red-pen passes

1. Circle every assumption

Mark anything that depends on a flight arrival, attraction opening time, train reservation, or provider terms. These are the places where “suggested” must remain visibly different from “confirmed”.

2. Draw the route, not just the attractions

Put each hotel, station, neighbourhood, and major activity on the same page. If a family has to cross the city repeatedly, the problem is the shape of the route. A good plan makes that shape legible before anyone books.

3. Cross out one thing per day

Remove the least important block from each day and check whether the remaining route still works. A plan that survives a late start, rain, or a tired child is more useful than one that only works on paper.

Where Alfred fits

Validation is a planning aid, not a booking guarantee: confirm opening hours, availability, fares, and partner terms with the relevant provider.

Start with the plan, red-pen the assumptions, then decide what is ready to book.
