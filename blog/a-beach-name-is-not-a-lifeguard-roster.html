A beach name is not a lifeguard roster

In Byron Bay, a beach with a famous name can go most of the year without a lifeguard. In late November one beach is on the roster. Plan the swim days around the roster, not the postcard.

2026-11-10 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

Byron Shire Council’s 2026–27 roster lists nine beaches. Eight are patrolled only from 18 December 2026 to 27 January 2027, and some also for about two weeks in April 2027. One, Byron Bay Main Beach, is patrolled all year.

Each beach on the roster keeps its name all year. Its lifeguards don’t. A family that picks a beach from a guide and a date from a calendar can land on a beach that has no one watching.

The signal: the roster is a calendar

On 9 November 2026 we read the Council’s lifeguard page, the ByronBay.com guide to Main Beach, Visit NSW’s Byron Bay and Brunswick Heads pages, and NSW National Parks pages for Cape Byron and Brunswick Heads Nature Reserve.

The Council lists three lifeguards at Main Beach on five days a week, all year, from 9 am to 5 pm on each service day. ByronBay.com says the Australian Lifeguard Service covers weekdays and Surf Life Saving volunteers cover weekends and public holidays. The Council says volunteers patrol “during the 2026 to 2027 season” and doesn’t define it.

Clarkes Beach, The Pass, Wategos and Tallow Beach are on the roster only for 18 December to 27 January and 10 to 25 April. Visit NSW says Main, Clarkes and Wategos are patrolled year-round, which disagrees with the Council. We followed the Council because it gives dates.

What breaks when the name is the plan

The postcard beach: The Pass and Wategos are the beaches guides name. In a week such as 23 to 27 November, by our reading of the roster, neither has lifeguards. The date, not the beach, decides whether anyone is on duty.

The hours: 8 am and 6 pm are outside the Council’s published standard service hours of 9 am to 5 pm, even at Main Beach. Extra patrols may operate, including during Schoolies. The standard hours do not prove whether anyone is on duty: check the flags and seek lifeguard advice when you arrive; if no red and yellow flags are up, don’t swim.

The nearby beach: NSW National Parks says the beaches in Brunswick Heads Nature Reserve aren’t patrolled and can have strong rips. The Council lists three emergency beacons at unpatrolled blackspots: the South Head rock wall at Brunswick Heads, Kendall Street at Belongil and Clifford Street at Tallow Beach. A beacon calls for help. It isn’t a lifeguard.

The flags: ByronBay.com says the red and yellow flags show the supervised area and that, if there are none, you should not swim. The Council says to swim between them and never alone.

Four checks before a swim

Date: is it inside the dates the Council lists for that beach? If the beach isn’t on the roster that day, treat it as unpatrolled.

Time: is it between 9 am and 5 pm on a day the roster covers?

Flags: are the red and yellow flags up when you arrive? If not, don’t swim, whatever the roster says.

Adult: who is in the water with the youngest child, and who is watching from the sand? Decide before you leave the car.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list the Council or Surf Life Saving publishes.

Where Alfred fits

Alfred plans from a travelling profile, so the ages it plans for come from the travellers on that profile. On web Lets Go, Holiday and Multi-city both take a day pace: Slow fills only an afternoon slot, Moderate a morning and an afternoon, and Active adds an optional evening.

Alfred doesn’t check lifeguard rosters, flags or closures while it writes the day. When the itinerary is ACTIVE, you can edit an item’s title, times and notes, so “swim at Main Beach only, between the flags, lifeguards 9 am to 5 pm on weekdays” can sit on the card.

In Hey Alfred, you can ask to swap two full days, and you confirm before it is applied. Our Byron Bay family itinerary puts both swims at Main Beach on a Monday and a Friday and keeps the other three days out of the surf.

Practical next step

Before you fly, write each planned swim with a date, a beach and a time. Check each against the Council page. Where the beach isn’t on the roster, move the swim to Main Beach on a weekday, or swap it for a pool or a walk.
