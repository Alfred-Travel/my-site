A fire danger rating is a four-day forecast

For a family heading into a national park in the warmer months, the fire danger rating is a planning input, not a morning-of check: it is forecast four days out, it belongs to a district, and on the worst days it closes the park.

The Victorian Government says fire danger ratings are forecast up to four days in advance and updated daily. Parks Victoria says that on a day of forecast Catastrophic fire danger, parks and forests close, and you may not receive a personal warning or see signs that the park is closed.

The fix isn’t to avoid the bush all summer. It is to read the forecast early, know which district you are going to, and agree in advance what each rating means for the trip.

The signal: the rating arrives before the day does

We read the Victorian Government’s plan and prepare page, Parks Victoria’s pages on being bushfire aware and Catastrophic forecast park closures, the Wilsons Promontory visitor and accommodation guides, and the Bureau of Meteorology’s fire weather services page on 2 November 2026.

The Victorian Government says Victoria has nine weather districts, each given its own rating for each day over the warmer months. Wilsons Promontory is in the West and South Gippsland fire district, according to Parks Victoria.

The Bureau of Meteorology lists four levels and ties each to an action: Moderate, plan and prepare; High, be ready to act; Extreme, take action now to protect life and property; Catastrophic, for your survival, leave bushfire risk areas.

What breaks when the rating is a morning-of check

The car is already packed: Parks Victoria says the Prom is closed on Catastrophic days, and that anyone already in the park should leave the night before or as early as possible in the morning. A check at breakfast is too late for that.

The wrong district: each district gets its own rating, so the rating where you live may not be the rating where you are going. Parks Victoria says to check fire danger ratings, fire restrictions and Total Fire Bans for your route and your destination.

Waiting for a sign: Parks Victoria says not to expect an official bushfire warning, and not to expect a ranger or emergency services to tell you when to leave. On Extreme days it says to reconsider plans to visit parks and forests.

Dinner on a camp stove: a Total Fire Ban is a separate, legal restriction declared by the CFA, the Victorian Government says. At the Prom, Parks Victoria says liquid-fuel and gas camp stoves aren’t allowed on Total Fire Ban days, though built-in gas cookers in built accommodation may be used.

Four checks in the four days before you leave

District, forecast, trigger, kitchen

Name the district: find your destination’s fire district before you book, and the districts on your route. For Wilsons Promontory, that is West and South Gippsland.

Read the forecast daily: start four days out and check again each evening, on the VicEmergency app, at emergency.vic.gov.au or on the VicEmergency Hotline, 1800 226 226.

Agree the trigger: Catastrophic means don’t go, or leave the night before. Extreme means reconsider. Decide now which day you would drop or swap.

Plan a no-flame dinner: on a Total Fire Ban day, check what you are allowed to cook with where you are staying. At the Prom, solid-fuel barbecues and fires aren’t allowed at any time.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list the Victorian Government, Parks Victoria or the Bureau publishes. Parks Victoria also says mobile reception at the Prom is unreliable, so download the VicEmergency app and set a watch zone before you lose signal.

Where Alfred fits

Alfred plans from a travelling profile, so the ages it plans for come from the travellers on that profile. On web Lets Go, Holiday and Multi-city both take a day pace: Slow fills only an afternoon slot, Moderate a morning and an afternoon, and Active adds an optional evening.

Alfred doesn’t check fire danger ratings, Total Fire Bans or park closures while it writes the day. When the itinerary is ACTIVE, you can edit an item’s title, times and notes, so “check West and South Gippsland rating tonight; Catastrophic means we leave” can sit on the card.

In Hey Alfred, you can ask to swap two full days, and you confirm before it is applied. Our Wilsons Promontory from Melbourne itinerary keeps a loose fourth day so a summit walk can move, and checks the rating before each drive. Book opens a partner checkout. It doesn’t know the fire danger.

Practical next step

Four days before you leave, look up the fire danger forecast for your destination’s district and for your route. Write the trigger on the first day card: what the family does if the rating reaches Extreme or Catastrophic. Then check again every evening until you are home.
