AI travel planner comparison methodology

Compare planners by usefulness, editability and trust—not by unsupported claims that one tool is universally best.

2026-09-22 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

A fair comparison gives each planner the same traveller brief, constraints and route, then records what the traveller can inspect and change.

Use one traveller brief

Test Alfred, Mindtrip, Layla, Stippl and Wanderlog with the same family profile, dates, pace, budget preference and multi-city route. Preserve the prompt, date, product version and output so the result can be repeated.

Score the decisions that make a trip work

  1. Constraint capture: are people, dates, pace and preferences carried through?
  2. Route coherence: does the sequence respect geography and transfers?
  3. Day-level pacing: are timing, effort and recovery visible?
  4. Editability: can one stop or constraint be changed without losing the route logic?
  5. Sharing: can the travelling group review the plan?
  6. Confirmation boundary: are live facts, availability and partner terms clearly separated?
  7. Recovery: does the plan offer a sensible fallback when weather or energy changes?

Report results without overclaiming

Use dated observations and quote the relevant output. A single run can show a useful pattern; it cannot prove universal product superiority, current availability or a permanent ranking.

Alfred’s testable promise

Alfred should make profile, pace, itinerary, editing and sharing visible as one path. Measure planning intent separately from successful generation, an ACTIVE itinerary and booking-hub intent.

What remains to be tested

The comparative results are intentionally not asserted in this draft. The next version should include the same brief, dated outputs, limitations and a clear explanation of what each tool did or did not expose.
