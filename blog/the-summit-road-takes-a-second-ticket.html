The summit road takes a second ticket

A day plan that says “drive up Cadillac Mountain” hides a second purchase. Acadia’s entrance pass covers the park; the summit road needs a timed vehicle reservation of its own, released on a schedule and not sold at the gate. Four checks before the drive goes into a family plan.

A national park day in a trip plan usually reads as a place and a time: Cadillac Mountain, late afternoon, summit views. At Acadia, in Maine, the National Park Service treats the road to the top as its own booking. The pass on your windscreen gets you onto the Park Loop Road. It doesn’t get your car up the mountain.

One park, two purchases

The National Park Service (NPS) says an entrance pass is required for every part of Acadia, all year. In 2026 a private vehicle pass was US$35, valid for seven days, and non-US residents aged 16 and over paid an extra US$100 each unless they had an Annual or America the Beautiful pass.

Cadillac Summit Road is different. From 20 May to 25 October 2026, NPS required a separate vehicle reservation to drive it, at US$6 per vehicle. Reservations are sold online in advance through Recreation.gov, and NPS says they aren’t available at the park. No other road in the park needs one.

The reservation runs on a release clock

In 2026, NPS released 30 percent of the Sunrise and Daytime reservations 90 days ahead and the other 70 percent two days ahead, both at 10 am Eastern time. So a family that misses the first release still has a second chance, but only two days out, at a set hour.

For travellers in Australia, by our calculation, 10 am Eastern in July is midnight in Sydney or Melbourne. If the two-day release falls while you’re still at home, it is a midnight job. If you’re already in Maine, it is a mid-morning one, best done before the morning’s walk takes you out of phone range.

A slot is an entry window, not a booking for the view

Daytime reservations have a 30-minute entry window and Sunrise reservations a 90-minute one. In July 2026, the Sunrise window opened at 4 am. Once inside, you can stay until 9 pm, when the road closes to vehicles, but there is no re-entry and no assigned parking space at the summit.

Two more rules catch people at the gate. Vehicles over 21 feet long, bike racks and hitch attachments included, are turned away even with a reservation. And NPS asks you to print or save the confirmation’s QR code before you arrive, because connectivity in the park is unreliable.

The free Island Explorer bus, which covers much of the park, doesn’t go up Cadillac Mountain. NPS says no reservation is needed to reach the summit on foot, by bike or by taxi, but for most families with young children, the car is the plan.

Four checks before the summit goes in the plan

1. Two tickets. Buy the entrance pass and the Cadillac reservation as separate items, and count the non-resident fee if you aren’t a US resident.

2. Release time. Note when the 90-day and two-day releases fall in your own time zone. Set an alarm for the one you’ll use.

3. Entry window. Plan the day around arriving inside the window, not around the view. A 30-minute window after a long beach morning is easy to miss.

4. The car. Check its length with any bike rack, save the QR code offline, and decide what the day looks like if you don’t get a slot.
