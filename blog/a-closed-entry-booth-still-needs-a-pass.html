A closed entry booth still needs a pass

For a family driving to Jervis Bay, an empty entry booth at Booderee can look like a free day in the park. It isn’t. The pass has moved online and onto your dashboard, and the place to fix a missing one is a WiFi spot, not a booth.

Booderee National Park says its entry station is currently closed. It asks visitors to buy a park pass online before arriving, print it, and display it on the dashboard. Parks Australia says compliance officers patrol the park and check that visitors have a valid pass.

The fix isn’t to look for someone to pay at the gate. It is to buy the pass at home, print it before you leave, and know where to go if you arrive without one.

The signal: the booth is closed, the pass is not

We read Booderee’s park passes, pass FAQ, entry station closure, traffic changes, getting here and phone coverage pages, and Parks Australia’s booth closure notice, on 5 November 2026.

Parks Australia’s notice of 31 July 2026 says the entry station booths are closed for maintenance, and that visitors must buy a pass before or during their visit. Booderee’s notice of 9 October 2026 says the entry station is still closed, and that rangers may ask to see a valid pass during your visit.

Booderee lists a one-day vehicle pass at A$20, a one-day concession pass at A$10, a one-year pass at A$80 and a two-year pass at A$110. It says walkers and cyclists enter free, and that a camping booking includes park entry.

What breaks when the booth is treated as the plan

The old instruction: Booderee’s park passes page still says that if you can’t buy online, you can buy a pass from the entry station. Its newer closure notices say the station is closed. Read the news page, not only the fees page.

The phone signal: Booderee says mobile and internet coverage in the park is very limited. A plan to buy the pass on a phone at the beach may not work. Parks Australia names one fix: WiFi at Green Patch, strong enough to buy a pass online.

The screen: Booderee asks for a printed pass on the dashboard or windscreen with the QR code visible, and says it is your responsibility to display it. A pass on a phone in someone’s pocket isn’t on the dashboard.

The detour: without a pass, Booderee sends drivers to its Visitor Centre or to its mobile van at the Jervis Bay Supermarket on Village Road. Its traffic notice of 9 October 2026 says inbound traffic is diverted through the visitor car park during works, and to allow extra time.

The wrong park: the White Sands Walk and Hyams Beach are in Jervis Bay National Park, which NSW National Parks runs. Booderee’s pass rules are for Booderee. Shoalhaven Tourism says Hyams Beach has very limited parking, so the parking plan there is different too.

Four checks for a Booderee day

Buy, print, display, fallback

Buy before you leave: one one-day vehicle pass for each park day, or a concession pass if someone in the car is eligible.

Print at home: one copy per car, with the QR code clear.

Display on arrival: on the dashboard or windscreen, QR code up, before you park.

Know the fallback: the Visitor Centre, the mobile van at the Jervis Bay Supermarket, or WiFi at Green Patch.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list Booderee or Parks Australia publishes. Booderee asks visitors to check its news section to keep up to date.

Practical next step

The night before you drive to Jervis Bay, count your Booderee days, buy that many one-day vehicle passes online and print them. Put them in the glovebox, not the suitcase, and add a note to the park day saying where the WiFi is.
