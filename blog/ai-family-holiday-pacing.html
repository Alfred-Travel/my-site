Family holiday pacing: the decisions an AI planner should expose

A family itinerary is useful when it protects the day’s energy. The plan should show what happens first, how long a transfer takes and what changes when the weather or a child’s energy changes.

A family plan should reduce coordination work without pretending that every day will go exactly to schedule.

Start with the family, not the destination list

Capture children’s ages, walking tolerance, nap needs, interests, accommodation base, preferred start time and willingness to change hotels. Those constraints shape the route more than a longer attraction list.

Use one anchor and one flexible option

Give each day a named main decision, a realistic sequence and a nearby fallback. A shorter day can be the better day when it protects a transfer or keeps the group from spending its energy on logistics.

Protect transfers and meal decisions

Make airports, trains, ferries and hotel changes visible. Name the neighbourhood or route logic for a meal stop without implying that a table is reserved or that opening hours are guaranteed.

A practical day pattern

  • 09:00: depart from the stated base after breakfast.
  • 09:30–11:30: named, low-friction anchor near the first route segment.
  • 11:30–12:00: protected transfer or reset.
  • 12:00–13:15: meal area selected for route coherence.
  • Afternoon: one named fallback if energy or weather changes.
  • 17:00: return to base before the evening decision.
