A missile test closes the dunes, not the day

“We’ll do White Sands on Friday” sounds settled until the park’s closures page lists a test. White Sands shares a boundary with a working missile range, and while it tests, the only road into the dunes shuts. The fix isn’t a spare day. It’s a spare few hours, and knowing which road you’re on.

Most park closures are about the park: weather, fire, a washed-out road. At White Sands National Park in New Mexico, the National Park Service (NPS) also closes the dunes for its neighbour. White Sands Missile Range runs military tests, and while some are under way, NPS shuts Dunes Drive, the only road into the dunefield, for visitor safety.

Up to three hours, sometimes on a day’s notice

NPS says Dunes Drive may be closed for up to three hours during tests, and that all activities along it are prohibited while it is shut. Park staff are usually told well in advance, it says, but notice from the range can arrive as little as 24 hours ahead. NPS lists upcoming closures on its Military Testing Park Closures page.

When we read that page on 18 November 2026, it listed two tests, on 12 and 16 November, each from 7 to 8 am, with US Highway 70 blocked as well. Two dates are not a pattern, so we wouldn’t plan around early mornings being the risky slot. What matters is the size of the window: hours, not days.

The visitor centre stays open

A test closure doesn’t shut everything. NPS says White Sands Visitor Center and its gift shop stay open during military test closures. The visitor centre is open 9 am to 5 pm, with a museum, a 17-minute film and junior ranger books, and the gift shop sells and rents the waxed plastic saucers families use to sled the dunes. An hour there is a plan, not a lost morning.

The road there can close too

NPS says US Highway 70 between the park and Las Cruces may also close during tests, and gives a White Sands Missile Range number for closure information: (575) 678-1178. Its operating hours page describes the closure as US 70 between Alamogordo and Las Cruces. Either way, a family driving from El Paso through Las Cruces could meet a roadblock before reaching the park.

NPS gives two routes from El Paso, each about an hour and a half: through Las Cruces on Interstate 10 and US 70, or north on US 54 to Alamogordo, then 15 miles west. Our inference: on a test day the Alamogordo side is the better approach, but check the closure details rather than assume it is open. NPS also warns that a GPS given only “white sands” may route you to the missile range.

Sunset is the other closing time

White Sands isn’t open around the clock. NPS says the gates open at 7 am every day and close 30 minutes after local sunset, or an hour after it in summer, which ran from 22 May to 7 September in 2026. Its ranger-led Sunset Stroll is timed to end at sunset, and NPS says it can be cancelled for heat, weather or missile range activities.

Four checks before White Sands goes on the day card

1. The schedule. Read the closures page the evening before and again that morning; notice can be as short as 24 hours.

2. The window. Give the dunes two slots, such as a morning and the next morning, so a three-hour closure moves the visit instead of cancelling it.

3. The road. Know which highway you are driving in on and whether the test blocks it. Even from Alamogordo, check US 70.

4. The gate. Look up tonight’s sunset and the closing time it sets, and be driving out, not sledding, when it comes.
