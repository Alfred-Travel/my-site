The park address is not the park gate

Typing a national park’s address into the car feels like the safe choice. At Mount Rainier, the National Park Service says the street address leads to its headquarters building, that GPS may pick the wrong one of five entrances, and that rerouting can take hours.

Most park-day planning is about what happens inside the entrance: the walk, the viewpoint, the picnic. The drive there gets one line, usually an address pasted into a phone. At Mount Rainier National Park in Washington State, the National Park Service (NPS) warns that this line is where the day can go wrong, and gives a separate address for the Nisqually Entrance, the only entrance it keeps open to cars all year.

What NPS says about the address

The park’s directions page, read on 24 November 2026, says the street address for Mount Rainier National Park, 55210 238th Avenue East, leads to the park headquarters in Ashford, not to the Nisqually Entrance. To reach the entrance by GPS, it gives 39000 State Route 706 E, Ashford, WA 98304, and recommends road maps as well as GPS units. The park’s basic information page repeats the warning.

The same directions page says the park has five entrances, that GPS may take drivers to the wrong one or try to follow the most direct route towards closed gates, and that it can take hours to reroute to the correct entrance. NPS’s advice is to check directions and maps, not just GPS.

Why it costs more with children in the car

Our inference: at a mountain park, the wrong entrance isn’t around the corner, and the roads between entrances are seasonal. NPS says Stevens Canyon Road, State Routes 410 and 123, and the Sunrise and Ohanapecosh areas close in mid-October or early November and don’t reopen until summer. It also says Carbon River and Mowich Lake can’t be reached from SR 165 at all while the Fairfax Bridge is closed.

Then there is the queue. NPS says waits at the Nisqually Entrance can be over an hour on the busiest summer weekends and holidays, and that the Paradise car parks fill by late morning at weekends. Our inference: an hour lost to the wrong entrance can also cost the parking space that the early start was meant to win.

Four checks before a park drive

1. Which entrance. Name the entrance your day starts from, not just the park. For Longmire and Paradise at Mount Rainier, NPS’s directions point to the Nisqually Entrance in the southwest corner.

2. Which address. Use the address the park gives for that entrance on its own directions page, and check the route against a map before you leave. At Mount Rainier that is 39000 State Route 706 E, Ashford, not the headquarters address.

3. Whether the road behind it is open. NPS says to always check the road status page before a trip, because unexpected closures can happen at any time, and to check the alerts too. An entrance that is open may still lead to a road that isn’t.

4. How you navigate with no signal. NPS suggests downloading the park’s section of the NPS App for offline use, and having another destination in mind if the entrance queue is long. Our suggestion, not an NPS rule: do the download the evening before, while you still have wi-fi.
