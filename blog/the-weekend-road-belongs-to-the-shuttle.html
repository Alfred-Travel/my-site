The weekend road belongs to the shuttle

“We’ll drive up to Moro Rock on Saturday” sounds like a plan. In Sequoia National Park in summer, it is a plan for a road your car can’t use that day. The same road can be open to you on Thursday and closed on Saturday, so the day of the week belongs in the plan.

Most road rules in national parks are about the road: snow, chains, length limits. Some are about the calendar. In Sequoia National Park, the National Park Service (NPS) says the road to Moro Rock, Tunnel Log and Crescent Meadow is closed to general motor vehicle traffic on summer weekends and holidays when the free shuttles are operating. On those days the shuttle is the way in.

What NPS says about the road

The NPS road conditions page, read on 20 November 2026, says that in summer the Crescent Meadow Road and Moro Rock loop are most easily reached by free shuttle, especially on weekends and federal holidays, and that the roads are closed to general motor vehicle traffic on summer weekends and holidays when the shuttles run. The vehicle length page says the same road is narrow, with heavy traffic from vehicles and shuttles in summer.

The shuttle page lists Route 2 from Giant Forest Museum to Moro Rock and Crescent Meadow, with Tunnel Log just past Moro Rock. A family who wrote “drive to Moro Rock” on a Saturday has a day card that the road won’t honour.

Why the weekday matters

The rule depends on two things: the day of the week and the shuttle season. In 2026, NPS ran summer shuttles from 22 May to 7 September. When we looked, the 2027 dates weren’t posted, so a July 2027 plan can only assume they will be similar and check again. Holidays count as well as weekends, and NPS lists the Fourth of July weekend among its busiest times.

The car park fills before the plan starts

Even on a shuttle day, the bus has to start somewhere. NPS says the car parks at Giant Forest Museum and the General Sherman Tree are usually full by mid-morning, and as early as 10 am in the busy season, and points drivers to the larger lots at Wolverton and Lodgepole. It also says entrance lines can be long on summer Fridays, Saturdays and holidays, and that arriving before 8 am helps.

NPS also says you are likely to lose phone signal about five minutes inside the entrance, and suggests downloading its app’s park data beforehand. Our inference: decide which car park you are aiming for before you reach the gate, not when the first one is full.

Four checks before a park road goes on a weekend day card

1. The calendar. Is the day a weekend or a holiday, and is the shuttle season running on that date? Check the park’s shuttle page for the current year, not last year’s.

2. The car. Check the vehicle limits. NPS prohibits single vehicles longer than 22 feet, and anything towing, on the Moro Rock and Crescent Meadow road, so a campervan may be shut out on any day.

3. The parking. Choose a large lot that a shuttle route serves, and get there early. Move food and scented items into a bear box before you leave the car, as NPS asks.

4. The last bus. Ask the driver when the last pickup is. NPS suggests riding out and walking back, so missing the last shuttle can’t strand you.

Practical next step

The worked example is our Sequoia and Kings Canyon five-day family itinerary from Three Rivers. It puts the General Sherman Tree on a weekday, makes Saturday the Moro Rock shuttle day from Lodgepole, and keeps a plan for a Saturday without shuttles.
