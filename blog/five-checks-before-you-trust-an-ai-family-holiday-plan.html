An AI holiday plan can look finished in seconds. Before your family trusts it, check five things: who picked the stay, what the flights are tied to, what the price really is, how full each day is, and what the planner won't do.

The signal

When we checked family and AI planning searches on 5 and 6 October 2026, the AI answers didn't link to planners' own pages. They cited roundups that test planners and report what each one actually shows. That's the right instinct for families too. Judge a plan by what it shows you, not by how confident it sounds.

Why a generated plan looks more finished than it is

A full itinerary arrives with a hotel, flights, activities and meals all filled in. Nothing on the page tells you which choices were yours, which are estimates, and which are just a starting point. With children, those are the parts that decide whether the trip works.

The five checks

Five checks before you trust the plan

1. Did you choose the hotel, or just receive it? A plan should show you the stay it picked and let you keep it or choose another before the rest is built around it.

2. Are the flights tied to that hotel? Flights and stay are one decision for a family. Check that changing one doesn't quietly break the other.

3. Is the price an estimate or a quote? A figure shown before booking is a guide. Find out what it's based on and treat it as a guide until a partner shows you a real fare.

4. Does each day match your family's pace? Count the activity slots per day. A plan built for adults can be too full for a five-year-old.

5. Does the planner say what it won't do? Every planner has limits. A trustworthy one states them, like how many cities it handles and what you can't change afterwards.

Where Alfred fits, and where it doesn't

Hotel first, while it plans. While your trip is still planning, Alfred shows We chose this hotel for you. Keep it, or choose from Other hotels.

Then the flights. Next comes Flights Selected for you: these flights stay with the hotel you chose. Keep them or choose others.

Activities and meals as they appear. You can browse Things we picked for you and Meals we picked for you. The Like button is only a marker on the card while you look. It isn't saved to the plan.

A better hotel, if there is one. Once activities and meals are in, Alfred may suggest a stay that suits the days better. It shows whether it costs more or less than your first pick, and Keep your first pick puts your original choice back.

An estimate, not a quote. On web Holiday search, Estimated costs (flights and hotels) is a rounded median of live flight + hotel packages. It is not a price you can book.

Day pace. Choose Slow (afternoon activity only), Moderate (morning and afternoon) or Active (adds an optional evening). Lunch and dinner are planned on all three.

Stated limits. Alfred plans Holiday, Multi-City and Road Trips. Multi-City takes up to five cities, each chosen from city search. Flights and hotels can't be swapped on a finished multi-city plan, so settle them while it plans.

Alfred doesn't take payment. Booking happens with the partner.

The next step

Before you plan, write down three things: the pace your youngest traveller can manage, the hotel area that matters most, and how many cities you really need. Then check the plan against them.
