A free-circulation day ticket is not a last-boat guarantee

A lake day pass says you may ride. It does not say the last boat still has room, or that the weather will let it sail.

Plan a trip Browse the blog

2026-10-16 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

A day card that says “free-circulation ticket, Bellagio and back” treats the pass as the plan. On Lake Como, Navigazione Laghi’s free-circulation ticket buys unlimited rides within the ports it covers while it is valid. It does not reserve a seat, set your boarding time, or keep the last boat running in a storm. Write the last return on the card, not just the ticket type.

The signal: unlimited rides, limited places

On 16 October 2026 we opened the operator’s pages for Lake Como. Navigazione Laghi’s English fares sheet (valid from 1 April 2026) says holders of free-circulation tickets may take an unlimited number of trips between the ferry ports the ticket was issued for, including intermediate stops, for as long as the ticket is valid. The ticket is personal and non-transferable. It can be issued for one day or six days. Fast services need the relative supplement, and night cruises are excluded unless something else is stated.

The same operator’s tickets page asks customers to go to the ticket office about 20 minutes before departure, notes that summer and holiday queues are common, and warns that more than one boat may sit at a dock — so follow staff instructions. It also says routes may be suspended in bad weather or strong wave motion. The timetable PDFs print a second hard line in English: places are limited to the maximum capacity of the boat.

When embarkation fails, the fares sheet is explicit. If there isn’t any room and embarkation is not possible, a traveller may request a refund of an unused ticket at the issuing office. That is a refund rule, not a promise that the next sailing will take you home before bedtime.

What breaks when the day pass is treated as the last boat

The late walk to the pier: a pass in your pocket does not shorten the queue. If the operator wants you at the ticket office about 20 minutes early, a card that only names the destination is already behind.

The wrong boat at a busy dock: when two vessels are alongside, boarding the first one you see can put you on the wrong line. The pass still validates; the timetable does not forgive the mistake.

The fast-service surprise: free-circulation tickets are valid on fast runs only with the supplement. Intermediate stops are not allowed on those tickets the way they are on ordinary boat runs.

The missing last-return minute: unlimited rides inside a zone are not the same as a guaranteed late sailing. Capacity can fill, and weather can suspend the route. Without a written last return and a no-boat fallback, the day has no exit.

Four checks before a ferry day goes on the plan

Treat the day pass as a boarding plan

Which zone the ticket covers: read the ports on the ticket and the stretch table, not a blog map. A free-circulation pass only covers the ferry ports it was issued for.

Which last return you will treat as hard: open the current timetable PDF for your season and weekday-or-holiday sheet. Write one return departure on the day card before you leave your base.

How early you need to be at the dock: plan to be at the ticket office about 20 minutes before that departure, and assume places are limited to the boat’s capacity.

What the day looks like if boats stop: weather and waves can suspend routes. Keep a lakeside or town-only version of the day that does not need a crossing.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a checklist Navigazione Laghi publishes as a family handout. Timetables and conditions change, so reopen the official tickets page and current PDF before you travel.

Practical next step

For every day on your plan that needs a lake boat, write three lines: which ticket zone you hold, which last return is the hard stop, and what the day becomes if boats are full or suspended. If any line is blank, the ferry day is still a hope. Fill it in before you leave the hotel.
