A free under-eighteen ticket is still a ticket

“Free for children” is a price, not a skip-the-desk rule. The family still needs the official ticket path, the adult who must accompany a younger child, and the closing time that applies to everyone.

A plan that writes “kids free” often stops there. On the ground, free under-eighteen admission still starts at a ticket office or an official ticket site. Treat the free ticket as a step with its own rules, not as proof that you can walk past the desk.

The signal: two Bilbao pages, same free line, different desks

On 9 October 2026 we opened two museum pages in Bilbao. The Guggenheim Museum Bilbao’s hours-and-admission page lists children and youths under 18 as free, asks visitors to buy on the museum’s official website, says no refunds are given for tickets already issued, and includes an audio guide with every ticket. Children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult. Admission desks close 30 minutes before closing time.

Itsasmuseum Bilbao’s useful-information page also makes under-18s free — and marks that free admission as available only at the ticket office. The museum is open Tuesday–Sunday 11:00–19:00 and closed on Mondays as a general rule. General admission is €10; reduced €5. Sundays from 17:00 are free for the general public.

Neither page treats free as invisible. Both still put the family in a ticket path.

What breaks when free is read as walk-up

The official channel: the Guggenheim page warns against ticket fraud and points visitors to its own site. A free child ticket bought somewhere else is not what that page describes.

The desk that still exists: Itsasmuseum’s free under-18 line is ticket-office only. Arriving without time for that desk turns “free” into a queue problem.

The adult rule: Guggenheim free admission for under-12s still requires an accompanying adult. The price is zero; the accompaniment rule is not.

The shared closing time: free tickets still clear the galleries with everyone else. A late free entry is still bounded by the desk’s last admission.

Four checks before you write “kids free” on the day

Treat free admission as a ticketed step

Where the free ticket is issued: official website, ticket office, or both. Write the channel on the day card.

What must be shown: age, ID, student proof, or an accompanying adult. Free is often conditional.

What is included: audio guide, timed entry, or nothing beyond the door. Do not assume extras.

When the desk stops: last admission and gallery clear times apply to free tickets too.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the museums’ own pages. They are not a single rule either museum publishes as a family handout. Hours and free-admission rules change, so re-open the page the week before you go.

Practical next step

Take every free child admission on your plan and fill in the four checks. If a line is blank, free is still a guess. Write the official channel and the accompaniment rule on the day card before the morning you go.
