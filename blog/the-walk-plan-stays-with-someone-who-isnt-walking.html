The walk plan stays with someone who isn’t walking

“We’ll be back by lunch” is a plan only if someone else knows it. In Lamington National Park, mobile coverage is unreliable and help can be hours away, so the person who raises the alarm has to be someone who isn’t on the track.

Most walk planning is about the walk: the distance, the grade, the time. One part of the plan has to stay behind. Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service (QPWS) asks walkers in Lamington National Park to leave their bushwalking plan with a friend, a relative, a reliable person or their accommodation, and says that person alone is responsible for contacting the police if the party is overdue.

What QPWS asks for

The Lamington safety page, read on 23 November 2026, lists what the plan should hold: your name and address, how many are in the party, their ages and any medical conditions; the car’s registration, make, model, colour and where it is parked; and the route, with expected departure and return times. The same page says rescue and medical help can be hours away, even by helicopter.

It also says mobile coverage in the park is not reliable, though depending on your provider you can often get a signal near lookouts. The statewide QPWS walking safety page adds that with no connectivity you cannot reach Triple Zero (000) from a mobile phone, including through the Emergency Plus app.

Why a short family walk still needs one

QPWS says to be prepared even on short walks. Its walking times are for people of average fitness in proper footwear, and it says to allow longer with children. The Gwongoorool track, which QPWS times at about 2 hours for 6 km with roughly 200 steep rock steps, will take longer with a seven-year-old. A walk plan covers the gap between the time on the sign and the time you get back.

QPWS also says not to start long walks after midday and to finish an hour before sunset, because the rainforest gets dark quickly, and earlier in winter. Our inference: set the return time on the plan with the sunset in mind, not the hopeful finish.

Four checks before a family walk leaves the car park

1. Who holds it. Someone who isn’t walking with you and can be reached all day: a friend, a relative or your accommodation, as QPWS suggests. Make sure they know the call is theirs to make.

2. What is in it. Everyone’s names and ages, any medical conditions, the car’s registration, make, model, colour and parking spot, the tracks in order, and the times you leave and expect to be back.

3. When it becomes a call. Agree a time after which they contact the police. Our suggestion, not a QPWS rule: allow some slack after your expected return, and set it before dark.

4. How you close it. Tell them as soon as you are back, so a late message doesn’t start a search. QPWS doesn’t spell this step out; it follows from who holds the call. It does say a search and rescue is costly and puts people at risk.
