The stinger suit goes on before the swim

In the Whitsundays and the rest of tropical Queensland, the stinger plan is made on land: where the family swims, what the children wear, where the vinegar is, and who watches for 45 minutes if someone is stung.

2026-10-28 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

The water at Whitehaven Beach looks harmless. In tropical Queensland, the risk you plan around is often one you won’t see. Surf Life Saving Queensland describes the Irukandji as a small, transparent box jellyfish, 1 to 2 cm across and usually not seen. Its sting can feel minor at first, with severe pain, headache, vomiting and sweating 5 to 40 minutes later.

The fix isn’t to stay dry. It is to decide where the family swims, put the suits on before anyone gets in, know the first aid, and plan for the places with no lifeguard and no phone signal.

The signal: the season, not the sky

We read the Queensland Government page on dangerous marine life, Surf Life Saving Queensland’s marine stinger and North Queensland safety pages, the Queensland Parks safety page for Whitsunday Islands National Park and Whitsunday Regional Council’s Airlie Beach Lagoon page, all live, on 28 October 2026.

The Queensland Government says marine stingers, including box jellyfish and Irukandji, are in tropical Queensland waters all year round, and that the risk is higher in the marine stinger season, November to May. Surf Life Saving Queensland gives the same months.

Its advice for that season is direct: always wear a stinger suit when entering the water, and swim only in designated stinger net enclosures. The Queensland Government adds: swim at patrolled beaches between the red and yellow flags, obey safety signs, and consider a full-body lycra suit.

On the islands, Queensland Parks is plain about it: no lifeguards, medical help can be hours away, and no mobile phone reception at Whitehaven Beach or Tongue Point. It also says children may be particularly affected by stings because of their size.

What breaks when the swim is planned at the beach

The suit stays in the bag: Queensland Parks says to wear stinger suits, full-body Lycra, or protective clothing when snorkelling or entering the water. A suit that is packed but not worn does nothing.

A calm day is read as a safe day: the Irukandji is usually not seen, and stingers are present all year. Clear water and an empty beach don’t rule them out.

The sting looks minor: Surf Life Saving Queensland says someone who seems stable can develop severe symptoms in the following 30 minutes, and that a person stung in tropical waters should stay out of the water and be watched for 45 minutes.

The vinegar is somewhere else: the advice is to call Triple Zero (000) and douse the sting with vinegar for at least 30 seconds. Queensland Parks says not to use fresh water or rub sand into the welts, and to use sea water if the vinegar runs out.

The phone is the plan: at Whitehaven Beach and Tongue Point there is no mobile reception. Before you land, ask the boat crew how they would call for help.

Four checks before anyone gets in

Place, suit, vinegar, watch

Choose the water: in Airlie Beach, swim in the lagoon, which Whitsunday Regional Council says is completely safe from stingers and marine wildlife all year round. In the sea, look for a patrolled beach with flags and a stinger net.

Suit everyone first: full-body suits on, children included, before anyone goes in. Check they fit before the trip, not on the sand.

Know where the vinegar is: find it before you swim, whether it is in your bag, on the boat or at the beach. If someone is stung: out of the water, call 000, vinegar for at least 30 seconds.

Agree the watch: one adult stays with anyone who is stung, out of the water, for 45 minutes, even if they say they feel fine.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a list any of these agencies publishes. Queensland Parks also warns that sharks can be in Whitsunday waters at all times of the year and crocodiles at most times of the year, and says never to swim or wade at dawn, dusk or night.

We didn’t confirm a stinger net at Airlie Beach itself. Our Whitsundays plan therefore treats the lagoon as the swim and the sea as a suits-only place.

Practical next step

Find the water days on your plan. On each card, write where the family will swim, whether there is a net or a lifeguard, who carries the suits and the vinegar, and who does the 45-minute watch. Then check the suits fit before you fly.
