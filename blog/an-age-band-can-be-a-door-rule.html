An age band can be a door rule

A price list says what a child pays. Sometimes the same row says the child can’t come in at all.

Plan a trip Browse the blog

2026-10-19 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

A family day card that says “Hypogeum, 10.00, four tickets” reads every age row as a price. At Malta’s Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum, one of those rows is not a price. Heritage Malta’s ticket table lists “Infants (1–5): Not admitted” in the same column as the fares. Before an anchor goes on the plan, read each age band as one of three things: a door rule, a price, or a rule about where the ticket is sold.

The signal: one table, three kinds of rule

On 19 October 2026 we opened Heritage Malta’s pages for the Hypogeum. The site page lists adults (18+), youths (12–17), senior citizens (60+) and children (6–11) with prices, then “Infants (1–5): Not admitted” on the next line. The ticket store page spells it out: for their own safety, children under 6 cannot be admitted. The terms add that children aged 6 to 13 must be accompanied by an adult. Heritage Malta’s access statement, a separate 2022 document, gives that band as 6 to 15. When two official documents disagree, plan to the stricter reading and confirm it when you book.

The same pages explain why a missed age rule is expensive. Tours take 10 visitors at a time and start strictly on time. Visitors should be on site 15 minutes early, and the terms say tickets are not refunded or exchanged. The access statement says tickets are usually booked around six weeks ahead.

Malta is a useful worked example because the age bands move from site to site. On official pages on 19 October 2026, St John’s Co-Cathedral in Valletta lets children under 12 in free with an adult, and staff may ask for documentation for a child ticket. At the Saluting Battery, the child ticket covers ages 5 to 15 and the adult ticket starts at 16. Malta Public Transport’s Explore Child Card covers ages 4 to 10, and children under 4 travel free.

On the Gozo Channel ferry, children aged 3 to 12 pay a child fare sold only at the Mġarr booths; infants aged 0 to 2 travel free but still need a free ticket validated before boarding. At Ġgantija, Ħal Tarxien and Fort St Elmo, Heritage Malta lists infants aged 1 to 5 as free.

That is a four-year-old who rides the bus with a child card, enters St John’s and Ħal Tarxien free, and cannot go into the Hypogeum at all.

What breaks when every age row is read as a price

The child at the door: the family arrives with four tickets in mind and one child below the minimum. Without a split plan written in advance, one adult may end up outside with that child, holding a ticket the terms say won’t be refunded.

The child ticket you can’t buy ahead: on Gozo Channel, the child fare is sold only at the Mġarr booths. A plan that says “ferry tickets bought online” is wrong for the child, even if it is right for the adults.

The free child who still needs a ticket: “free” is not the same as “no ticket”. Gozo Channel asks for an infant ticket to be collected and validated, even at no charge.

The proof of age you didn’t pack: St John’s may ask for documentation when you choose the under-12 ticket. If it sits in the hotel safe, the free ticket becomes a problem at the door.

Four checks before a family anchor goes on the plan

Read each age band before you count tickets

How old each child will be on the day: write the age each child will be on the travel date, not today. A birthday between booking and travel can move a child across a band.

Whether the row is a door, a price or a desk rule: for every paid anchor, mark each age row as “not admitted”, “pays this price”, or “ticket bought or validated somewhere specific”.

Who must stay with whom: note any accompaniment rule, such as the Hypogeum’s adult requirement for young children, and use the strictest wording you find.

What the split looks like: if anyone is below a minimum, write the other half of the morning now. In Valletta, that can mean one adult takes the Hypogeum tour while the other takes the younger child to Ħal Tarxien nearby, where Heritage Malta lists infants aged 1 to 5 as free.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the pages above. They aren’t a checklist Heritage Malta or the transport operators publish. Age bands, prices and terms change, so reopen the official pages before you book.

Practical next step

For every paid anchor on your plan, write one line per child: their age on the day, whether the site admits them, and where their ticket is bought or checked. If any line is blank, the anchor still needs work. Fill it in before you pay.
