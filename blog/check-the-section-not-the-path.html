Check the section, not the path

A famous walk can be one name on the map and several sections on the ground, each with its own status. Plan the walking day by the sections you will actually walk, and by the alert that can close them all.

2026-10-23 · Alfred Team · Travel notes

The Cinque Terre’s coastal path is one line on the map. On the national park’s trail list it is four numbered sections between Riomaggiore and Monterosso, 592-1 to 592-4, each tagged SVA2. On 23 October 2026 those four sections carried three different labels: closed, expert and walkers’ grade. A family told “the coastal path is open” could be right about one stretch and wrong about the next.

The fix is not a better map. It is the label on each section you plan to walk, read close to the day, and a check of the weather alert that can close every section at once.

The signal: one path, three labels

We checked the Cinque Terre National Park’s trail list, trail pages and news page, and the Via dell’Amore booking site. On 23 October the list showed Riomaggiore–Manarola, the Via dell’Amore, and Manarola–Corniglia as closed. Corniglia–Vernazza was graded for experts, at about 1 hour 30 minutes, and Vernazza–Monterosso for walkers, at about 2 hours. A day earlier, the list had the Via dell’Amore as tourist-grade and Vernazza–Monterosso as expert.

The label slot holds two kinds of information. Where a section is closed, it reads Chiuso, closed. Elsewhere it shows a difficulty grade. So the list tells you a section is for experienced walkers; it does not, on its own, tell you every part of it is open.

The trail pages add detail, and don’t always match the list. On 23 October the Via dell’Amore’s page still read tourist-grade with no weather alert; the Vernazza–Monterosso page still read expert. That page also notes a Monterosso ordinance of 24 August 2026 closing its Servano stretch, with access via Via Corone. A park notice of 10 September 2026 had closed Corniglia–Vernazza–Monterosso pending geological checks. We found no reopening notice, so the notice and the list don’t clearly agree.

Even the shortest section has its own rules. When open, the Via dell’Amore needs a booked slot with limited places, runs one way from Riomaggiore to Manarola, and from 26 October 2026 takes its last entry at 5.30 pm. The official pages differ on details: one describes 15-minute slots for 100 people, others 30-minute slots for 200, and the length appears as 1.112 km and as 900 metres.

Above all of that sits the alert. On 20 October 2026, after ARPAL issued an orange weather alert, the park closed its whole trail network from 5 pm until 6 am the next morning and suspended card sales. Two days earlier, for a yellow alert, it asked visitors not to walk its trails and those already out to return to the villages.

What breaks when the plan reads the path name

One open stretch stands in for the whole path: Vernazza–Monterosso being graded for walkers said nothing about Riomaggiore–Manarola or Manarola–Corniglia, which were closed.

A grade is read as a green light: a section graded for experts may suit two fit adults and not a seven-year-old, and the grade doesn’t show a local ordinance.

The notice and the list disagree: a closure notice and a later list don’t always line up, so the plan needs to know which to trust and who to ask.

The alert is missed: a section label doesn’t show a same-day orange alert that closes every trail.

The ticket is treated like a train: the booking site says Via dell’Amore tickets can’t be changed, though a late arrival can use the first slot with space.

Four checks before a walking day goes on the plan

Read each section, then the day

Name the sections: write down the numbered sections the day walks, not the path’s name.

Read each label and page: closed, tourist, walkers’ or expert grade on the park’s list, then open each section’s own page for ordinances and partial closures.

Check the alert that morning: the park posts weather alerts on its news page and points to the Allerta Liguria portal for updates.

Keep a train version: give every walking day a version that uses only trains, and a flexible day next to it.

The four checks are our recommendation, built from the park’s pages above. They aren’t a checklist the park publishes. Labels, ordinances and alerts change, so reopen the official pages before you go, and ask a park Infopoint when two pages disagree.

Where Alfred fits

Alfred plans from a travelling profile, so the ages it plans for come from the travellers on that profile. On web Lets Go, Holiday and Multi-city both take a day pace: Slow fills only an afternoon slot, Moderate a morning and an afternoon, and Active adds an optional evening.

Alfred doesn’t check trail labels, ordinances, weather alerts or Via dell’Amore slots while it writes the day. When the itinerary is ACTIVE, you can edit an item’s title, times and notes, so “Via dell’Amore, 592-1 only; check the park’s list and alerts at breakfast; train if closed” can sit on the card.

In Hey Alfred, you can ask to swap two full days, and you confirm before it is applied. A day with a flight can’t be swapped that way. Our Cinque Terre from La Spezia itinerary takes the villages by train and keeps a swap day like this. Book opens a partner checkout. It doesn’t know which section is closed.

Practical next step

Find the walking day on your plan. On its card, write the numbered sections you will walk, where their labels are posted, where alerts are posted, and the train you take instead. If a section reads closed, change the route, not the hope.
