# Website V2 — Homepage Rewrite

## Source of truth

The homepage should feel like the public-facing version of the Alfred webapp.

The current webapp language and interaction patterns to carry across are:

- Explore the world, YOUR way.
- Lets Go
- Trips
- Profile
- Settings
- Download The App
- Plan trip
- Family
- Mid Range
- travel dates
- interest filters such as Shopping, Food, Kids Friendly, Nightlife, and Beach
- destination and airport route chips
- visible trip constraints and choices

The website should explain these product behaviors without inventing a separate visual identity.

---

## Homepage job

The homepage must answer four questions quickly:

1. What is Alfred?
2. Who is it for?
3. How does it help me plan a real trip?
4. What should I do next?

The page should move visitors toward the webapp with one clear action: plan a trip.

---

## Recommended page architecture

1. Header and product CTA
2. Hero: explore the world your way
3. Product preview: turn choices into a trip
4. How Alfred works
5. Why the webapp feels different
6. Real trip examples
7. Trust and support
8. App CTA
9. Global footer

---

# 1. Header

## UI recommendation

Match the webapp’s clean navigation and keep the header lighter than the current marketing navigation.

Recommended navigation:

- Product
- Trips
- Compare
- Blog
- Support
- Plan a trip

Use one primary CTA label consistently:

> Plan a trip

Avoid switching between:

- Start Exploring
- Download Alfred
- Start Your Journey
- Discover More

The CTA can route to the webapp or app download depending on device, but the public label should remain consistent.

---

# 2. Hero

## Visual recommendation

Use a direct product-led composition:

- left: headline and CTA
- right: webapp-style trip planning preview
- dark surface matching the app
- visible selections such as Family, Mid Range, dates, interests, and destinations
- no generic destination stock image as the primary visual

The preview should look like Alfred’s real planning interface, not a decorative marketing card.

## Recommended hero copy

### Eyebrow
> Alfred Travel

### Headline
> Explore the world, your way.

This deliberately carries the webapp’s existing language into the public website.

### Supporting copy
> Build a trip around the people travelling, the places you want to see, and the pace that works for you. Alfred turns those choices into a clear, editable itinerary you can take toward booking.

### Primary CTA
> Plan a trip

### Secondary CTA
> See how it works

### Supporting trust line
> Family planning · Multi-city routes · Editable itineraries

## Do not use

- Start your journey
- Discover your next adventure
- Your next adventure starts here
- generic AI travel planner claims as the main headline

---

# 3. Product preview section

## Objective
Show the website visitor what the webapp actually feels like.

## Recommended heading
> Start with what matters to your trip.

## Supporting copy
> Tell Alfred who is travelling, how you want the trip to feel, and what you want to do. Your preferences stay visible as the plan takes shape.

## UI content to show

Use the same terminology visible in the webapp:

- Family
- Mid Range
- Select travel dates
- Kids Friendly
- Food
- Shopping
- Beach
- Plan trip
- destination and airport selections

## Suggested caption
> Choose the people, pace, dates, and interests that shape the trip.

## Visual rule
The product preview should be a real or carefully cropped webapp screen wherever possible. Avoid abstract illustrations and generic feature icons.

---

# 4. How Alfred works

## Recommended heading
> From your choices to a trip you can use.

## Recommended four-step structure

### Step 1 — Set the trip up
> Choose your travellers, dates, pace, and interests.

### Step 2 — Build the route
> Add destinations and shape the journey around the places that matter.

### Step 3 — Review the plan
> See the itinerary as a structured trip you can understand and adjust.

### Step 4 — Keep moving
> Share it, refine it, and take it toward booking when the plan feels right.

## UI recommendation
Use the webapp’s actual product states or screenshots for each step. Each step should show one meaningful action, not a generic icon plus paragraph.

---

# 5. Why Alfred feels different

## Recommended heading
> Less searching. More trip.

## Supporting copy
> Alfred keeps the important decisions in one place, so planning feels more like shaping a trip and less like rebuilding one across tabs, chats, and spreadsheets.

## Recommended benefit blocks

### Your way
> Start with the destinations, interests, and pace that matter to you.

### Your people
> Plan around the family, group, or travel companions coming with you.

### Your trip flow
> Keep the route, timing, and itinerary connected as the plan changes.

### Your next step
> Move from a good idea to a plan you can review and book with confidence.

## Avoid

- Translator & synthesiser
- Counter-narrative to bias
- Autonomous decision support
- other internal/technical labels as primary customer-facing benefits

Those concepts can be used in deeper product documentation, but the homepage should speak in traveller language.

## Supporting copy

Each card should include:

- traveller type
- pace
- short planning insight

# 7. Trust and support

## Recommended heading
> Plan with more clarity before you commit.

## Supporting copy
> Alfred is designed to make the important travel decisions easier to see: who is travelling, how the days flow, where the route bends, and what still needs checking before booking.

## Trust cues

Use visible, concise statements:

- Editable itineraries
- Family and multi-city planning
- Route and timing context
- Partner booking when you are ready
- Support Center
- Help & FAQ

## Recommended support copy
> Need help with your account or trip? Visit the Support Center or browse Help & FAQ.

## Avoid

- unsupported security superlatives
- “highest security standards” unless evidenced
- model-name claims in the trust section
- sending users to social channels for normal support

# 8. App CTA

## Recommended heading
> Ready to plan your next trip your way?

## Supporting copy
> Start with the people, places, dates, and interests that matter. Alfred will help turn them into a trip you can work with.

## Primary CTA
> Plan a trip

## Secondary CTA
> Download the app

On mobile, keep a persistent but unobtrusive CTA:

> Plan a trip

Do not use a generic `Download` label without explaining what the app does.

# Homepage content decisions

## Keep

- structured itineraries
- family and multi-city planning
- booking pathway
- app download
- itinerary examples
- visible support

## Rewrite

- homepage hero
- feature section headings
- CTA labels
- section introductions
- support language
- metadata and social copy

## Remove or de-emphasise

- generic travel clichés
- model names in primary copy
- internal AI concepts as customer-facing benefits
- blockchain/reward language on the homepage
- “Travel 3.0” language
- volume claims unless they are current and strategically useful

---

# Acceptance criteria

The homepage rewrite is ready for implementation when:

- the visitor understands Alfred within one screen
- the hero uses the same language as the webapp
- the product preview is recognisably the webapp
- every CTA uses consistent language
- the page explains people, pace, destinations, and trip flow
- support is visible without returning to the homepage
- the content sounds like a traveller product, not an AI marketing template
- the page works as the entry point to the wider Website V2 system
