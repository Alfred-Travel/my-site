Sample plan

Six days with a real Fukuoka–Nagasaki handoff

One primary anchor per day, a protected transfer day, and alternatives for weather, hills, fatigue, and late arrivals.

Day 1 — Arrival in Fukuoka · choose the right base

01
Route decision

Start with the family’s arrival time and luggage, not an attraction list. Hakata is the practical rail-and-arrival base; Tenjin can suit a family prioritising central shopping and food. Keep the first evening around the chosen base and protect sleep.

Day 2 — Fukuoka city cluster · Ohori or Hakata

02
Route decision

Choose one cluster: Ohori Park and the Fukuoka Castle ruins for an open-air, lower-pressure day, or Hakata/Kushida-area food and shopping when heat or rain makes a compact route better. Do not cross the city for a second anchor.

Day 3 — Dazaifu choice · half-day or protected rest

03
Route decision

Dazaifu can be the cultural half-day if the family wants a shrine-and-approach-street experience. If the children are tired or the weather turns, keep the day in Fukuoka and use a nearby indoor or food-led alternative instead of forcing the excursion.

Day 4 — Fukuoka → Nagasaki · protected rail handoff

04
Route decision

Treat the move as a travel day. Build the live JR Kyushu itinerary around the relay and Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen connection where applicable, reserve seats only after checking the current service, and avoid a timed Nagasaki attraction immediately after arrival.

Day 5 — Nagasaki · choose a hill-light route

05
Route decision

Choose one main story: Peace Park and the Atomic Bomb Museum area, or Dejima and the waterfront, then keep the second block close by. Glover Garden and hillside movement can be rewarding but add steps and weather exposure; keep a lower-effort alternative visible.

Day 6 — Departure buffer · preserve the handoff

06
Route decision

Use the actual departure airport and train/road connection to decide the final base. Confirm luggage, station access, and live departure timing; Alfred should leave the final morning editable rather than promising one more attraction.

Planning notes

Decisions to settle in Alfred

Hakata or Tenjin?

Choose Hakata when rail access and arrival simplicity matter. Choose Tenjin when central food, shopping, and city time matter more. The right answer depends on arrival, luggage, and the family’s preferred walking load.

Rail handoff

JR Kyushu shows Northern Kyushu coverage and the Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen corridor. Check the current service, connection, seat reservation, and luggage plan before committing.

Nagasaki hills

Peace Park, Dejima, the waterfront, and Glover Garden create different walking and step profiles. Pick the story that fits the family rather than trying to collect every landmark.
