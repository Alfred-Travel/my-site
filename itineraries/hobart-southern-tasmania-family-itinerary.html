Day-by-day plan

Day 1 — Arrive in Hobart: settle near the waterfront

01

Keep the first day intentionally small. Transfer to a central Hobart base, unpack, and take a short waterfront walk around Salamanca and Constitution Dock if energy allows. Choose an early dinner with a simple return route to the hotel. Pacing logic: Do not schedule Mount Wellington or a long drive after a flight. The first win is a low-friction arrival and a good night’s sleep.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 2 — Hobart essentials with a flexible afternoon

02

Spend the morning at the Tasmanian Museum and Art Gallery or another central indoor option, then walk the waterfront and Salamanca area. Leave the afternoon open for a rest, playground stop, or a second short activity. Family logic: A flexible half-day absorbs jet lag, weather changes, and the reality of travelling with different energy levels.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 3 — kunanyi / Mount Wellington and a short city evening

03

Check the mountain forecast and road conditions before leaving. If conditions are good, drive or transfer to kunanyi / Mount Wellington for the views and a short, age-appropriate walk. Return to Hobart for a relaxed afternoon and a meal near the accommodation. Validation boundary: Wind, snow, ice, cloud, or road closures can change the plan. Keep a Hobart museum, gallery, or food route as the same-day fallback rather than treating the summit as guaranteed.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 4 — Richmond and the Coal River Valley

04

Take a short regional outing to Richmond. Pair the historic village with a family-friendly lunch and one additional stop, not a full list of attractions. Return to Hobart before the evening becomes a second travel day. Route logic: Richmond is useful because it creates a meaningful change of scene without requiring a hotel move. Keep the route compact and confirm opening hours for the chosen stops.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 5 — Tasman Peninsula day or overnight transfer

05

Choose between two versions: Low-packing version: Make the Tasman Peninsula a long day trip only if the family is comfortable with the drive and the main attraction timing works. Lower-stress version: Move to a Tasman Peninsula base for two nights, stopping at a suitable viewpoint or short walk on the way. Do not combine every peninsula landmark in one day. Pick one anchor—such as a coastal viewpoint, a short accessible walk, or a wildlife experience—and keep a weather fallback.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 6 — Peninsula nature and a protected afternoon

06

Use the morning for the selected peninsula anchor. Keep the afternoon deliberately light: local beach time, a short walk, a café, or downtime at the accommodation. If the weather is poor, replace the outdoor anchor with a shorter outing and preserve the evening. Trust logic: The itinerary should state what is optional. A family plan is stronger when it has room to remove an activity without breaking the whole route.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 7 — Return to Hobart and depart

07

Allow a conservative transfer buffer back to Hobart or the airport. If the departure is late, choose one nearby activity only after checking luggage storage, road conditions, and the required airport arrival time.
