Hobart and Southern Tasmania Family Itinerary: 7 Days at a Calm, Flexible Pace

A flexible southern Tasmania family route with Hobart as the anchor, realistic regional days, and room for weather, ferry, and energy changes.

Logistical validation

A calmer route with visible fallback choices

Keep Hobart as the default anchor, protect the arrival and departure buffers, and confirm road, ferry, weather, park, and attraction details before committing.

  • Recommended length: 7 days / 6 nights
  • Default bases: 4 nights Hobart, 2 nights Hobart or 2 nights Tasman Peninsula depending on family tolerance for packing
  • Pace: One main outing per day, with a lighter arrival and recovery day
  • Best for: Families wanting food, wildlife, easy nature, and short-to-moderate drives
  • Key planning boundary: Check road conditions, ferry schedules, park access, weather, and attraction opening times before departure

Day-by-day outline

Day 1 — Arrive in Hobart: settle near the waterfront

Keep the first day intentionally small. Transfer to a central Hobart base, unpack, and take a short waterfront walk around Salamanca and Constitution Dock if energy allows. Choose an early dinner with a simple return route to the hotel.

Pacing logic: Do not schedule Mount Wellington or a long drive after a flight. The first win is a low-friction arrival and a good night’s sleep.

Day 2 — Hobart essentials with a flexible afternoon

Spend the morning at the Tasmanian Museum and Art Gallery or another central indoor option, then walk the waterfront and Salamanca area. Leave the afternoon open for a rest, playground stop, or a second short activity.

Family logic: A flexible half-day absorbs jet lag, weather changes, and the reality of travelling with different energy levels.

Day 3 — kunanyi / Mount Wellington and a short city evening

Check the mountain forecast and road conditions before leaving. If conditions are good, drive or transfer to kunanyi / Mount Wellington for the views and a short, age-appropriate walk. Return to Hobart for a relaxed afternoon and a meal near the accommodation.

Validation boundary: Wind, snow, ice, cloud, or road closures can change the plan. Keep a Hobart museum, gallery, or food route as the same-day fallback rather than treating the summit as guaranteed.

Day 4 — Richmond and the Coal River Valley

Take a short regional outing to Richmond. Pair the historic village with a family-friendly lunch and one additional stop, not a full list of attractions. Return to Hobart before the evening becomes a second travel day.

Route logic: Richmond is useful because it creates a meaningful change of scene without requiring a hotel move. Keep the route compact and confirm opening hours for the chosen stops.

Day 5 — Tasman Peninsula day or overnight transfer

Choose between two versions:

  • Low-packing version: Make the Tasman Peninsula a long day trip only if the family is comfortable with the drive and the main attraction timing works.
  • Lower-stress version: Move to a Tasman Peninsula base for two nights, stopping at a suitable viewpoint or short walk on the way.

Do not combine every peninsula landmark in one day. Pick one anchor—such as a coastal viewpoint, a short accessible walk, or a wildlife experience—and keep a weather fallback.

Day 6 — Peninsula nature and a protected afternoon

Use the morning for the selected peninsula anchor. Keep the afternoon deliberately light: local beach time, a short walk, a café, or downtime at the accommodation. If the weather is poor, replace the outdoor anchor with a shorter outing and preserve the evening.

Trust logic: The itinerary should state what is optional. A family plan is stronger when it has room to remove an activity without breaking the whole route.

Day 7 — Return to Hobart and depart

Allow a conservative transfer buffer back to Hobart or the airport. If the departure is late, choose one nearby activity only after checking luggage storage, road conditions, and the required airport arrival time.

Optional Bruny Island swap

Bruny Island can replace Day 4 or Day 6 for families who want a ferry-and-wildlife day. Treat it as a separate route decision, not an automatic add-on. Check ferry times, vehicle requirements, weather, and the family’s tolerance for a long day before committing. A late return should not be followed by an early flight.

Family and transfer notes

  • Keep Hobart as the default base to reduce packing and preserve flexibility.
  • If adding the Tasman Peninsula overnight, move once rather than changing accommodation every night.
  • Put the longest drive after a settled night, not immediately after arrival.
  • Plan one anchor activity and one optional activity per day.
  • Keep an indoor or short-route fallback for every outdoor day.
  • Confirm accommodation location against the actual day sequence; a cheaper room far from the route can create more daily transfer cost and fatigue.
  • Confirm road, ferry, park, weather, and opening information close to travel.
