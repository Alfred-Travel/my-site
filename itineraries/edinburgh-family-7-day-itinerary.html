Day-by-day outline

Day 1 — Arrival, check-in, and a short Old Town reset

  • Arrival logic: leave room for airport transfer, luggage, check-in, and weather. Edinburgh Airport tram, bus, or taxi can work depending on bags and family energy.
  • Base logic: choose one hotel/apartment base for the full city stay where possible; avoid changing accommodation for a short trip.
  • Afternoon: Royal Mile edge, a short closes-and-viewpoints loop, or Princes Street Gardens if energy allows.
  • Evening: early meal near the base; no timed attraction on arrival day.
  • Trust note: confirm current attraction hours, weather, and any access restrictions before departure; this draft is a route structure, not a live booking confirmation.

Day 2 — Castle anchor and Old Town cluster

  • Morning anchor: Edinburgh Castle with timed entry if required.
  • Lunch: remain in the Old Town corridor rather than adding a cross-city restaurant.
  • Afternoon: choose one lighter option—museum, gardens, a short viewpoint walk, or rest at the base.
  • Family pacing: castle slopes, cobbles, and queues can make this a full day; do not stack another major timed attraction.

Day 3 — New Town, gardens, and a flexible rainy-day branch

  • Morning: Princes Street Gardens and a New Town walking loop.
  • Anchor option: National Museum of Scotland or another indoor museum selected for the family’s interests.
  • Afternoon: break, café, or short neighbourhood exploration.
  • Rain fallback: keep the indoor anchor and shorten outdoor walking; do not treat bad weather as a failed itinerary.

Day 4 — Arthur’s Seat decision day

  • Morning decision: Arthur’s Seat only if weather, footwear, and family energy support it. Otherwise use Holyrood Park’s easier paths or a museum/indoor alternative.
  • Midday: lunch around the Holyrood/Southside corridor.
  • Afternoon: return to the base for recovery, then a low-commitment evening.
  • Safety/pacing: avoid presenting the summit as mandatory; a flexible route is more trustworthy for families.

Day 5 — Water of Leith and Stockbridge at family pace

  • Morning: Water of Leith section or Dean Village viewpoint, selected for conditions and mobility needs.
  • Lunch: Stockbridge area, with a short stop rather than a long multi-stop crawl.
  • Afternoon: playground, bookshop, gardens, or downtime.
  • Route logic: keep this as a neighbourhood day to offset the higher walking and queue load of the castle and Old Town.

Day 6 — Choose: Highlands day trip or nearby lower-friction route

  • Option A, Highlands: choose one operator and one realistic route after checking total drive time, pickup point, weather, and child tolerance. Do not promise that a long coach day fits every family.
  • Option B, lower-friction: a shorter outing reachable from Edinburgh with more control over return timing; keep the city base unchanged.
  • Booking boundary: attraction, rail, tour, and accommodation availability must be checked with the relevant provider under its own terms.

Day 7 — Buffer, favourite revisit, and departure

  • Morning: leave a buffer for packing and departure. Revisit one favourite nearby area or use a short café/garden block.
  • Departure logic: work backward from the flight or train time, allowing for airport transfer and luggage rather than adding a final distant attraction.
  • Repeat-use prompt: save the edited route as a reusable family template for a future Scotland trip or a different season.

Family, pacing, transfer, and route logic

  • One base is preferable for seven days unless a specific Highlands or regional plan justifies a change.
  • Cluster Old Town, Castle, Holyrood, New Town, Stockbridge, and Dean Village by day; avoid zig-zagging between them.
  • Build around one major anchor per day, with a flexible block after lunch.
  • Edinburgh weather can change the comfort and safety of exposed walks; every outdoor-heavy day needs an indoor or shorter branch.
  • The Highlands decision belongs on Day 6 because it is the trip’s largest transfer and energy risk, not because it is automatically the best use of time.
  • Use walking where the route is practical, then fill gaps with tram, bus, or taxi according to bags, hills, mobility, and weather.
  • This is an itinerary framework, not a claim that any attraction, transport service, or booking is currently available.
