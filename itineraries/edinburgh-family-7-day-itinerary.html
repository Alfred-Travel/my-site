Day-by-day plan

Day 1 — Arrival, check-in, and a short Old Town reset

01

Arrival logic: leave room for airport transfer, luggage, check-in, and weather. Edinburgh Airport tram, bus, or taxi can work depending on bags and family energy. Base logic: choose one hotel/apartment base for the full city stay where possible; avoid changing accommodation for a short trip. Afternoon: Royal Mile edge, a short closes-and-viewpoints loop, or Princes Street Gardens if energy allows. Evening: early meal near the base; no timed attraction on arrival day. Trust note: confirm current attraction hours, weather, and any access restrictions before departure; this draft is a route structure, not a live booking confirmation.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 2 — Castle anchor and Old Town cluster

02

Morning anchor: Edinburgh Castle with timed entry if required. Lunch: remain in the Old Town corridor rather than adding a cross-city restaurant. Afternoon: choose one lighter option—museum, gardens, a short viewpoint walk, or rest at the base. Family pacing: castle slopes, cobbles, and queues can make this a full day; do not stack another major timed attraction.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 3 — New Town, gardens, and a flexible rainy-day branch

03

Morning: Princes Street Gardens and a New Town walking loop. Anchor option: National Museum of Scotland or another indoor museum selected for the family’s interests. Afternoon: break, café, or short neighbourhood exploration. Rain fallback: keep the indoor anchor and shorten outdoor walking; do not treat bad weather as a failed itinerary.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 4 — Arthur’s Seat decision day

04

Morning decision: Arthur’s Seat only if weather, footwear, and family energy support it. Otherwise use Holyrood Park’s easier paths or a museum/indoor alternative. Midday: lunch around the Holyrood/Southside corridor. Afternoon: return to the base for recovery, then a low-commitment evening. Safety/pacing: avoid presenting the summit as mandatory; a flexible route is more trustworthy for families.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 5 — Water of Leith and Stockbridge at family pace

05

Morning: Water of Leith section or Dean Village viewpoint, selected for conditions and mobility needs. Lunch: Stockbridge area, with a short stop rather than a long multi-stop crawl. Afternoon: playground, bookshop, gardens, or downtime. Route logic: keep this as a neighbourhood day to offset the higher walking and queue load of the castle and Old Town.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 6 — Choose: Highlands day trip or nearby lower-friction route

06

Option A, Highlands: choose one operator and one realistic route after checking total drive time, pickup point, weather, and child tolerance. Do not promise that a long coach day fits every family. Option B, lower-friction: a shorter outing reachable from Edinburgh with more control over return timing; keep the city base unchanged. Booking boundary: attraction, rail, tour, and accommodation availability must be checked with the relevant provider under its own terms.

Confirm before booking: Check current schedules, access, weather, opening hours and supplier terms for this day.

Day 7 — Buffer, favourite revisit, and departure

07

Morning: leave a buffer for packing and departure. Revisit one favourite nearby area or use a short café/garden block. Departure logic: work backward from the flight or train time, allowing for airport transfer and luggage rather than adding a final distant attraction. Repeat-use prompt: save the edited route as a reusable family template for a future Scotland trip or a different season.
